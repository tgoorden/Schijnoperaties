## Mission

The iconographic research database **Fictional Healing** has been online since 2026. It is a scholarly iconographic resource dedicated to the study of fictional surgical procedures that emerged in late medieval Europe and continued to circulate throughout the early modern period.

Bringing together the surviving corpus of these motifs, the database documents the visual tradition of fictional healing in all its diversity. Each record includes object information, transcriptions, and a concise bibliography. By assembling these works in a single research environment, the database reveals the extensive textual and visual dialogue between individual images, making it possible to trace the transmission, transformation, and development of these iconographic traditions over time.

Designed as a research tool for historians of art, literature, medicine, religion, and late medieval and early modern culture, the database provides a foundation for the comparative study of recurring motifs, their interrelationships, and their historical development.

The project originated from the collection and research of **Wendy Wauters** and **Malcolm Jones**. As of September 2026, approximately one third of the corpus is available online; the remaining material will be added during 2026-2027. The corpus will continue to expand through the integration of related collections, with the aim of creating the most comprehensive dataset possible for the study of fictional healing imagery.

Comments, additional image submissions, and general enquiries are welcome at: [contact@fictionalhealing.be](mailto:contact@fictionalhealing.be)
